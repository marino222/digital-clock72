Impedance for RS485, step by step

From capacitors and inductors to termination resistors. Each step builds on the one before.

Short version

  • Impedance is how a part opposes AC. A transmission line has a single number for it, Z0.
  • Z0 only matters when a trace or cable is long compared with the signal edge. Then a mismatch sends a reflection back.
  • On your board that means 120 Ω at the two bus ends, short stubs, a tight A/B pair. No controlled-impedance trace and no impedance calculator for RS485.

01

Capacitor and inductor at DC

A capacitor resists a change in voltage. An inductor resists a change in current. They only fight changes, so once a DC source has been on for a while, both settle.

  • Steady state: a capacitor is an open circuit (a gap). An inductor is a short circuit (a wire).
  • Right at switch-on it is reversed: a capacitor acts like a wire, an inductor like a gap.
Common slip

A settled inductor does not give "unrestricted" current. It drops no voltage, and the rest of the circuit (R) still sets the current. A settled capacitor is not like a normal resistor either. It blocks DC completely.

02

Reactance: opposition that depends on frequency

With AC the source keeps reversing, so neither part ever settles. How strongly each one opposes current now depends on frequency. That opposition is reactance, X, measured in ohms.

XL = 2πfLinductor: grows with frequency
XC = 1 / (2πfC)capacitor: shrinks with frequency
Common slip

Frequency zero does not make both reactances zero. Only XL goes to zero. XC goes to infinity, which is exactly why DC cannot pass a capacitor.

03

Phase: voltage and current fall out of step

In a capacitor, current has to flow before the voltage can change, so current peaks first. In an inductor, voltage has to be applied before current can build, so voltage peaks first. Both gaps are 90°.

04

Impedance: resistance and reactance together

Impedance, Z, is the total opposition to AC current. Resistance R turns energy into heat. Reactance X stores it and hands it back. They sit 90° apart, so they combine like the two sides of a right triangle.

Z = R + jXa complex number, j is the imaginary unit
X = XL − XCthey point opposite ways and partly cancel
|Z| = √(R² + X²)φ = atan(X / R)
Common slip

Z = R + jX, not R + X. Z is a complex number, and X is a signed sum of XL and XC.

05

Why a DC board still has an AC problem

The supply is DC, but a logic signal is a square wave. A square wave is a sum of sine waves: a fundamental plus odd harmonics. The sharper the edge, the more high-frequency content it carries, and the more reactance and delay matter.

bandwidth ≈ 0.35 / triseexample: 5 ns edge → about 70 MHz

Edge rate sets the frequency content, not baud rate. Your bit time at 1 Mbaud is 1 µs, but the transceiver output can switch in a few nanoseconds.

06

Transmission lines and Z0

Every cable and PCB trace has inductance and capacitance along its whole length. Picture it as many tiny slices, each with a series inductor and a shunt capacitor. An edge has to charge each slice in turn, which is why signals travel at a finite speed.

Z0 = √(L′ / C′)characteristic impedance, in ohms
v = 1 / √(L′ · C′)propagation speed

Why one number is enough: for a low-loss line, Z0 is the same real value at every frequency. Every harmonic of your square wave sees the same Z0, so you do not need to calculate them one by one.

Common slip

Z0 is not a heater. The energy is not burned in the line, it goes into the line's fields and travels on. Z0 also does not depend on length. And 120 Ω belongs to the twisted-pair cable RS485 assumes, not to RS485 itself. Coax is typically 50 Ω.

07

Reflections: why termination exists

A wave on the line carries voltage and current in a fixed ratio: V / I = Z0. The line sets that ratio. Whatever sits at the end sets its own: V / I = Rload. If the two differ, the leftover has to go somewhere, and it goes into a wave travelling back.

  • Open endCurrent must be zero there. The reflected wave carries opposite current, which adds voltage. Voltage at the end doubles.
  • ShortVoltage must be zero there. The reflected wave carries opposite voltage.
  • R = Z0The wave already has the ratio the load wants. Nothing is left over, nothing reflects. This is termination.
  • Step in ZA connector or a change in trace geometry asks the wave for a different V / I. Part of it reflects, the rest continues with amplitude 1 + Γ.
Γ = (Rload − Z0) / (Rload + Z0)
Γ = (Z2 − Z1) / (Z2 + Z1)at a step
Vreflected = Γ · Vincident
08

Long or short: when it matters

A reflection only hurts if it arrives as a separate echo. If the round trip is much shorter than the edge, the echo merges into the edge and the receiver never sees it. Rule of thumb: treat a section as a transmission line when its one-way delay is longer than about one sixth of the rise time.

09

Your board: the RS485 rules

  • From your repo
    Daisy chain, A to A and B to B, 120 Ω at both ends, common ground between all nodes. (Bring-up notes, kickoff doc §1.6.)
  • From your repo
    1 Mbaud 8N1, 72 transceivers on one segment, DE and RE tied to one GPIO. (Kickoff doc §1.4, §1.5, §3.1.)
  • Recommendation
    One 120 Ω resistor per board behind a solder jumper, open by default. Close it only on the two end nodes.
  • Recommendation
    Failsafe bias, pull-up on A and pull-down on B, once at the master end. Take the values from the transceiver datasheet.
  • Recommendation
    Keep the connector-to-transceiver stub short, ideally under 3 to 5 cm. Route A and B together at equal length, with no extra vias, over an unbroken ground area.
  • Recommendation
    No controlled-impedance trace for RS485. Skip the JLCPCB impedance calculator for this net.
  • Open
    Termination and bias strategy: jumper, fixed, or switchable (kickoff Q3).
  • Open
    Unit load: can 72 transceivers share one segment? Parts rated 1/8 unit load allow up to 256, so check the datasheet (Q4).
  • Open
    Total bus length against baud rate. About 80% of bandwidth is spare, so lowering the baud rate is available if needed (Q5).
  • Open
    Auto-addressing scheme. If it needs a segmentable bus, termination and stub rules change (Q7).
  • Open
    Layer count. A ground area under A and B is easy on 4 layers. On 2 layers it needs a solid ground pour on the other side (Q13).
